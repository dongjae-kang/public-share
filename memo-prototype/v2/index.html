1 · The claim the market holds

On 11 October, eight producers holding more than 90 percent of China's effective polysilicon capacity met in Hangzhou and agreed that nothing would sell below 43,000 yuan a tonne. Five days later the November contract on the Guangzhou Futures Exchange closed at 34,285. The quote and the price sit 20 percent apart, and the association that publishes the quote counts two or three firms transacting at it. One of those two numbers is wrong.

Author's paragraph. What the equity market has priced on the floor: the move in the solar ETFs on the headlines, the sell-side line that effective capacity contracts by 28 to 40 percent, and the re-rating this implies for Tongwei, GCL and Daqo.

2 · Mechanism

A floor announced by private firms binds only if someone enforces it. The state has used three tools on this market in twelve months, and a fourth that it did not aim at the price. The record below is the market's reaction to each, measured on the contract that was live that day.

Four tools, one price

What the state did, when, and what the main contract did in the sessions after. December 2025 to January 2027.

Antitrust · 6 January 2026

A consolidation vehicle registered on 9 December was to buy and shut a third of surplus capacity. The main contract rose 11.6 percent in the next ten sessions and closed at 61,595 on 17 December. On 6 January the State Administration for Market Regulation barred the producers from any agreement on capacity, output or price. The contract fell 16.4 percent in five sessions and 46 percent by its 10 April low. The one tool the market believed was the one that removed a premium.

Price Law · 27 July to 11 October 2026

The association's cost model (27 July) and the regulator's Yancheng meeting (31 July) changed the legal theory: a cartel is illegal, and so is selling below cost. The September contract rose 19.9 percent in ten sessions; the November contract then gave back 15 percent, from 39,190 on 14 August to 33,255 on 15 October. The 43,000 floor set on 11 October was followed by a 4.9 percent fall in two sessions. No main contract has closed above 43,000 since 23 April.

Energy standard · published 27 June, in force 1 January 2027

GB 29447-2026 is the one tool in the sequence with the force of law: existing lines must run at 6.3 kilograms of coal equivalent per kilogram or close, against a 2025 industry average of 6.8. The contract rose 3.9 percent in the ten sessions after publication and was down 3.2 percent after twenty. Nameplate capacity above 3.5 million tonnes a year stands against output near 1.2 to 1.3 million, so most of what the standard retires is already idle.

Taxes and trade · 1 April and 4 December 2026

The export VAT rebate ended on 1 April: a record 68 GW of modules shipped in March, and exports are down 9.6 percent year on year through September. Washington's minimum import price of 0.38 dollars a watt applies from 4 December, against a China FOB price of 0.107. Neither moved the polysilicon contract for long (minus 10 percent in the five sessions after 1 April, plus 4.5 percent after the 16 October export-control draft). They moved volumes. The floors that bind are taxes.

What the floor rests on, then, is the Price Law's clause against selling below cost and a cost model written by the trade association. Converted without VAT, 43,000 yuan a tonne is about 5.32 dollars a kilogram. Daqo's second quarter put cash cost at 4.57 and total cost at 5.95. The floor clears cash cost and misses full cost, even for one of the lowest-cost producers. It is a cash-cost floor presented as a cost floor, and it is being held by not selling.

Author's paragraph. Why the floor broke in June when Daqo resumed sales below cost, and why five months of stock makes the same dilemma worse now.

3 · Winners and losers

The floors that bind are taxes. Beijing removed the export VAT rebate on modules on 1 April. Washington's Section 232 minimum import price of 0.38 dollars a watt takes effect on 4 December, against a Chinese FOB price near 0.107. The first shrinks China's export volume. The second sets the US price by law for anyone outside China, whatever happens in Hangzhou, and the flow it covers is a sliver of what China ships.

Author's paragraphs. First order: who is insulated (a US manufacturer with a 45 GW backlog and no polysilicon), who is exposed (a levered module exporter with Southeast Asian plants now under AD/CVD). Second order: wafer and cell makers who get cheap feedstock and cannot use it; buyers outside the US who still pay 0.107.
