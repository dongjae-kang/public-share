Seeing the Feed

What the public route to YouTube gives an independent monitor: a short companion to a policy memo

The memo asks the European Commission to set one standard for the public data that independent researchers can obtain from video platforms. To see what YouTube gives today, we sent real requests to its official Data API, the public route, on October 12 and 13, 2026, with one API key on the default quota, and kept YouTube’s answers as returned.

1.One night on the public route

On the news day of October 12, 2026, PRISM’s nightly pipeline read a fixed list of 485 US news channels through the Data API. Every request costs quota units, and one key gets 10,000 units a day by default.

2.What each item returns

The memo’s standard names four items of public data for video platforms. The requests and answers shown under each item are for one video, a MeidasTouch upload of October 12 from the story in section 3, recorded on October 13, 2026 at 22:39 UTC.

Video detailsReturned in full

One call returns every field the watch page shows: title, channel, time, length, views, likes and comment count, at 1 quota unit per 50 videos. The limit sits earlier: finding a channel by search is capped at 100 calls a day.

Caption textListed; the text is refused

The API lists the caption tracks a video has, made by the uploader or generated by the platform, at 50 units a video. A request for the text of a track answered 401: API keys are not supported, and the documentation requires permission to edit the video. The player plays the same track to anyone who watches.

View counts over timeOne reading per call

The view count is the count at the moment of the call. There is no history for a video one does not own: a series exists only if the researcher keeps polling, and nothing between two readings can be recovered. YouTube’s Developer Policies also limit storage, in section III.E.4.b: an API Client must not store statistics retrieved as Non-Authorized Data for more than 30 days.

Three recorded readings

Three separate calls: the first reading, the second 16.6 hours later, and the call shown under Video details. Nothing between them is returned. Counted from the first reading, III.E.4.b sets November 12, 2026 as the date by which these readings must be deleted or refreshed.

Public commentsWithin quota

A page of up to 100 comment threads costs 1 unit, newest first or top first. There is no total and no oldest-first order, so reaching a video’s first comments, where coordinated arrival shows, means paging to the end.

Request and recorded answer

Request, as sent

GET https://www.googleapis.com/youtube/v3/commentThreads?part=snippet&videoId=mMok2XA6Dmk&maxResults=5&order=time&textFormat=plainText&key=REDACTED

Answer, as recorded: HTTP 200, 1 quota unit

items_on_page: 5
has_next_page: true
pageInfo: {"totalResults": 5, "resultsPerPage": 5}
newest_published_at: "2026-10-12T16:38:38Z"
orders_available: ["time", "relevance"]

totalResults is the size of this page, set by maxResults=5, not the number of comments. The details call counts 5,374 comments on this video. No order starts from the first comment.

Beyond the four items. Why a video was shown, impressions and watch time are not shown to anyone and are not in the public API; only vetted access under Art. 40(4) reaches them. The standard stops at what every viewer can already see.

The full record covers eight videos and cost 466 quota units: api_record.json. Comment text and author names were not stored.

3.One story, route by route

The memo asks that each platform’s annual risk assessment respond to independent research filed through the European Digital Media Observatory (EDMO). The evidence such a filing would carry, built from one story collected on October 12:

Bipartisan backlash grows over Trump’s Russian diesel deal as Zelenskyy says Russia violated the energy ceasefire

208 items from 83 outlets: 198 YouTube videos and 10 podcast episodes, with 9,774,739 views at first reading.

Published on PRISM on October 13, 2026: the story as published.

A cross-outlet analysis of the story has four parts. For each, what the public route supported:

OriginSupported

The earliest item in the collection window came from Bloomberg Television on October 12; the API returns a publish time for every video. Which upload a viewer saw first is exposure data, outside the public route.

FramingHeadlines only

Each outlet’s title and description, which the API returns, show what it put first. A comparison of what was said in the videos would need the caption text the API lists and refuses.

SpreadSingle readings

Two channels, MeidasTouch and CNN, held about 31% of all views at first reading. By the second reading, 16.6 hours later, the story’s YouTube videos had grown from 9.77M to 12.68M views (+29.7%). The curve between the readings is not returned, and under III.E.4.b the readings must be deleted or refreshed after 30 days.

ReceptionSupported, within quota

In a sample of 34,685 comments on the 198 videos, one account posted the same comment on 6 videos across 5 channels within six minutes, between 16:01:51 and 16:06:13 UTC on October 12: Why isn’t Putin speaking for himself instead of letting Trump speak on his behalf? No copy had more than 11 likes. The videos came from Hook Global (two videos), CNN, BBC News, MS NOW and FRANCE 24 English.

This is one account, reported as observed, not a campaign. Whether it was part of anything larger is the question the platform’s own assessment would have to answer.

Of the four parts, two are fully supported by the public route, one is supported at the level of headlines, and one rests on readings the researcher must take and then discard.

At the API’s published costs, following all twelve outlets would take an estimated 98 units a day, 1.0% of the default 10,000 units. Busy channels can need more, as the US run showed (1,028 units measured against 613 estimated), but for Ireland quota is not the constraint; the three items below are.

What the pilot cannot obtain at any size

  • The text of any caption track. The API lists the tracks; the download call answered 401 and requires permission to edit the video.
  • Any reading older than the call. A six-month series exists only if the pilot polls every video it follows, and III.E.4.b requires each reading to be deleted or refreshed within 30 days.
  • The first comments on a video, cheaply. No oldest-first order and no total; the pilot pages to the end to see how a thread began.

Method and limits

Instrument: PRISM’s nightly pipeline, which reads a fixed list of 485 US news channels, and one YouTube Data API key on the default quota. US figures are from the news day of October 12, 2026 (04:00 UTC October 12 to 04:00 UTC October 13). The second view reading came 16.6 hours after the first; the eight-video record and the Irish probe were made on October 13. Quota units are counted by our client as calls made; the Google Cloud console was not consulted. No YouTube data was obtained outside the Data API; the podcast episodes in section 3 came from each show’s RSS feed. The figures cover one platform over two days, and the growth in views is one interval that says nothing about the shape between readings. Policy text is quoted as read on October 13, 2026. Data files: api_record.json (section 2), view_snapshots_aggregate.json (view readings), ie_probe.json (section 4) and facts.js (derived figures and the pipeline’s run-log counts).
