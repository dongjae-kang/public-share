What the public route to YouTube gives an independent monitor

Step 1 of the proposal asks the Commission to set a minimum data specification for video platforms: video metadata, transcripts where published, view counts over time, public comments. The pages here test that specification against YouTube with real requests, so that the gap the proposal describes can be seen rather than asserted.

Through the route YouTube sanctions, the Data API, a researcher gets one item in full (metadata), one up to a quota ceiling (comments), one only as a listing without the text (transcripts), and one not at all (view counts over time). The developer terms then forbid keeping the counts that are returned for more than 30 days. What every viewer sees on a watch page and what a researcher may take from it are two different sets.

One story, route by route

Step 2 asks the Commission to require each platform's annual civic-discourse assessment to respond to independent cross-outlet research filed through EDMO. Below is one such filing, built from a story collected on October 12, with each module marked by the route it needed and whether the sanctioned route provides it.
via the Data APIvia the API, single readings onlynot returned by the API
Cross-outlet briefCollection window Oct 12 to 13, 2026 · Public data only · Source card: prism-news.org

Bipartisan backlash grows over Trump's Russian diesel deal as Zelenskyy says Russia violated the energy ceasefire

Question
Which framings did the feed repeat, where did the story enter the news-video ecosystem, and did identical comments appear across outlets at once.
Datavia API
208 items from 83 outlets: 198 YouTube videos and 10 podcast episodes, 9,774,739 views at first reading. Video metadata and comments through videos.list and commentThreads.list; podcast episodes through RSS.
Originvia API
Earliest item in the collected window: Bloomberg Television, October 12. Publish timestamps are returned for every video.
Framingpartly
Three outlets selected for maximal dispersion among the 198 videos: BBC News led with Zelenskyy's statement that Russia hit a power plant after the ceasefire was declared; The Damage Report folded the deal into a multi-topic episode in which Trump "angers republicans" with a "fake ceasefire"; Forbes Breaking News put Trump's response to Republican criticism first. The quoted framings are titles and descriptions, which the API returns. The transcripts that would let the comparison go past headlines are listed by the API but not returned (demonstration 01, Q2).
Spreadsingle readings
The two largest channels, MeidasTouch and CNN, held about 31% of all views at first reading. The peak item was a MeidasTouch video at 1,008,850 views; by the second reading, 16.6 hours later, the story's YouTube videos had grown from 9.77M to 12.68M views (+29.7%).
Each reading is a separate call. The curve between readings, where the spread happens, is not returned, and under III.E.4.b readings must be deleted or refreshed after 30 days.
Receptionvia API, within quota
Comment sample of 198 videos, 34,685 comments. One account posted the same comment ("Why isn't Putin speaking for himself instead of letting Trump speak on his behalf?") on 6 videos across 5 channels within six minutes, with at most 11 likes on any copy. The most-liked comments in the sample sat on CNN (1,086 likes) and MeidasTouch (876 and 791). Returned at 1 unit per page of 100 threads. There is no oldest-first order, so reaching a video's first comments means paging to the end.
Limits
About 31% of views concentrate in two channels; comment opinion reflects a sample; developments after the window are out of scope; the single-account pattern is one account, not a campaign, and is reported as observed.
What the assessment would answer
Whether the platform's own systems saw the same comment land on five outlets' videos within minutes, and what, if anything, that triggered. Under Step 2 the annual civic-discourse assessment responds to the filing; today there is no obligation to.

Of the four modules that carry evidence, two are fully supported by the sanctioned route, one is supported at the level of headlines, and one rests on readings the researcher must take and then discard. That is the shape of independent research under today's access, and the reason Step 1 precedes Step 2.

The terms that bind the researcher

Six passages from YouTube's Terms of Service, the API Developer Policies and the Data API documentation, and the three texts that say who may ask.
YouTube Terms of Service, Permissions and Restrictions, item 3
"access the Service using any automated means (such as robots, botnets or scrapers) except (a) in the case of public search engines, in accordance with YouTube's robots.txt file; or (b) with YouTube's prior written permission"
The clause that puts transcripts, community posts and any collection outside the API off limits. The X decision found that a contractual ban of this kind cannot be applied to researchers covered by Art. 40(12). Exception (b) is the door Step 1 asks the Commission to open as a class.
API Developer Policies III.E.4.b
"To be clear, an API Client must not store statistics retrieved as Non-Authorized Data for more than 30 days."
View, like and comment counts for videos the researcher does not own are non-authorized data. A series kept for six months exceeds this ceiling as written.
API Developer Policies III.E.4.d
"API Clients may temporarily store limited amounts of Non-Authorized Data for as long as is necessary for the purposes of the API Client but not longer than 30 calendar days."
The same ceiling for every other field taken through the API, including titles and descriptions, unless the data is refreshed or deleted every 30 days.
API Developer Policies III.E.2.b
"Do not aggregate API Data or otherwise use API Data or YouTube API Services to gain insights into YouTube's usage, revenue, or any other aspects of YouTube's business."
Cross-outlet research measures how the platform is used. Whether that falls under this clause is decided by the platform, which is the uncertainty Step 1 asks the Commission to remove.
API Developer Policies III.E.6 and III.I.14
"You and your API Clients must not, and must not encourage, enable, or require others to, directly or indirectly, scrape YouTube Applications or Google Applications, or obtain scraped YouTube data or content." / "use any technology other than YouTube API Services to access or retrieve API Data, including to access any portion of any YouTube audiovisual content"
Together with item 3 of the Terms of Service, these close the only route to the text of a caption track for a video one does not own.
Data API quota, Getting started
"a default quota allocation of 100 search.list calls, 100 videos.insert calls, and 10,000 units per day combined for all other endpoints"; "All API requests, including invalid requests, incur at least a one-point quota cost." More is available through "the Quota extension request form for YouTube API Services."
One night of the US monitoring used 3,366 units for metadata and the comments of six stories. Covering every comment on a day's 5,889 videos would not fit.

Who may ask, in three texts

  • Art. 40(12) DSA. Access for "researchers, including those affiliated to not for profit bodies, organisations and associations, who comply with the conditions set out in paragraph 8, points (b), (c), (d) and (e)". Those points are independence from commercial interests, disclosure of funding, data security, and proportionality. No academic affiliation test, no residence test.
  • Google's DSA researcher programme page (transparency.google, read Oct 13, 2026). Lists the Art. 40(8) conditions and "affiliated with not for profit bodies, organizations and associations", then adds: "This programme is currently only available for researchers affiliated with an EU-based institution" and "Individual platform teams may have additional eligibility requirements beyond those listed here." Step 1 names the residence test as one the standard should remove; the X decision treated the bearing of the research on risks in the Union, not the researcher's seat, as the test.
  • YouTube Researcher Program (research.youtube, read Oct 13, 2026). Applicants must be "affiliated with an accredited, higher-education institution that can grant degrees"; the program is open in 60 countries, Ireland and 25 of the 27 EU member states among them (Croatia and Slovenia are not listed); "researchers will provide YouTube with free access to and use of all research based upon YouTube data".

Limits

  • One platform, two days. YouTube only. X, Meta and TikTok are not measured here.
  • Demonstration 01 is eight videos. The top video of each of the six October 12 stories and the two most-viewed Irish videos in the probe. The captions.download refusal was recorded on one track; the documentation states the same requirement for every video.
  • View growth is one interval. Readings 16.6 hours apart show that a series can only be built by polling; they say nothing about the shape between them.
  • Quota is counted by the client. The Google Cloud console was not consulted.
  • Terms are quoted, not interpreted. Whether cross-outlet research falls under III.E.2.b, and whether the X decision's reasoning transfers to YouTube, are questions for the Commission and the courts; the proposal asks for the first and assumes the second.
  • Estimates are marked. The planner's outputs are arithmetic on measured volumes and published costs; the measured US fetch cost 1,028 units where the same arithmetic gives 613, because high-volume channels need several playlist pages. Read planner outputs as a floor.

Scripts and outputs

  • record_api_views.py produced api_record.json (demonstration 01; comment text and author names removed).
  • probe_ie_channels.py and resolve_ie_search.py produced ie_probe.json and ie_search_resolved.json (demonstration 03).
  • poll_views.py took the second reading of 586 videos; view_snapshots_aggregate.json holds the per-story totals and the growth distribution.
  • The nightly fetch and comment collection are pipeline code in the team's repository (07_phase2a_24h_fetch.py, collect_comments.py).
