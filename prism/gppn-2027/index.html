01The route

What each item of the specification returns

Exhibit 2 of the proposal grades YouTube's sanctioned route, the Data API, against the four items of the specification. The matrix on the right adds the two other ways to read the same watch page: a person with a browser, and a program reading the page outside the API, which the Terms of Service forbid. Each step below is one row of that matrix, with the real request and the real answer for one of eight videos.

AVideo metadatareturned in full

videos.list returns every field the watch page shows: title, channel, time, length, views, likes and comment count, at 1 unit per 50 videos.

Finding the channel in the first place is the cost the API does not cover: search.list is capped at 100 calls a day.

BCaption textlisted, text refused

captions.list names the tracks that exist for a video, uploader-made and auto-generated, at 50 units a video. captions.download answers 401: API keys are not supported, and the documentation requires permission to edit the video.

The player plays the same track to anyone. "Caption text the platform lists" in the specification covers both kinds: the uploader's track is published by the outlet, the auto track by the platform, and both are public on the page.

CView counts over timeone reading per call

statistics.viewCount is the count at the moment of the call. There is no history endpoint for a video you do not own; a series exists only if the researcher keeps polling, and nothing between two readings can be recovered.

The Developer Policies then set a ceiling on keeping what was read: III.E.4.b: "an API Client must not store statistics retrieved as Non-Authorized Data for more than 30 days."

DPublic commentswithin quota

commentThreads.list returns a page of up to 100 threads for 1 unit, newest first or top first. There is no total and no oldest-first order, so reaching a video's first comments, where coordinated arrival shows, means paging to the end.

Some channels turn comments off: 35 of the 1,049 videos whose comments the pipeline collected on October 12 (3.3%), and 8 of the 85 videos in the Irish probe.

EBeyond the specificationno route

Community posts are shown on every channel page but have no endpoint. Why a video was shown, impressions and watch time are not shown to anyone and are not in the public API; only vetted access under Art. 40(4) reaches them.

The specification stops at what every viewer can already see. That is the floor Step 1 asks the Commission to guarantee.

02One story, route by route

The filing EDMO would receive

Step 2 asks the Commission to require each platform's annual civic-discourse assessment to respond to independent cross-outlet research filed through the European Digital Media Observatory. Below is one such filing, built from one story collected on October 12, with each module marked by the route it needed and whether the sanctioned route provides it.

Three questions a platform's own risk assessment would have to answer about the same story, if Step 2 were in force. Each maps to one row of the matrix in chapter 01: framing needs caption text, origin and spread need timestamps and view counts over time, reception needs public comments.

  2. Where did the story enter the news-video ecosystem?
  3. Did identical comments appear across outlets at once?

    ESpreadsingle readings

    The two largest channels, MeidasTouch and CNN, held about 31% of all views at first reading. The peak item was a MeidasTouch video at 1,008,850 views. By the second reading, 16.6 hours later, the story's YouTube videos had grown from 9.77M to 12.68M views (+29.7%).

    Each reading is a separate call. The curve between readings, where the spread happens, is not returned, and under III.E.4.b readings must be deleted or refreshed after 30 days.

      GLimits, and what the assessment would answer

      Under Step 2, the annual civic-discourse assessment would respond to this filing: whether the platform's own systems saw the same comment land on five outlets' videos within minutes, and what, if anything, that triggered. Today there is no obligation to.

      • Concentration. About 31% of views sit in two channels, so the framings of the long tail weigh less than their count suggests.
      • Sample. Comment opinion reflects the comments collected, not every comment posted.
      • Window. Developments after October 13 are out of scope.
      • One account. The repeated comment is one account, not a campaign, and is reported as observed.
      • Headlines. Framing rests on titles and descriptions; the caption text that would deepen it is refused.

      Of the four modules that carry evidence, two are fully supported by the sanctioned route, one is supported at the level of headlines, and one rests on readings the researcher must take and then discard. That is the shape of independent research under today's access, and the reason Step 1 precedes Step 2.

      What the pilot cannot obtain at any size

      • caption textThe text of any caption track. captions.list names the tracks at 50 units a video; captions.download answered 401 ("API keys are not supported by this API") and requires permission to edit the video.
      • view historyAny reading older than the call. A six-month series exists only if the pilot polls every video it follows, and III.E.4.b requires each reading to be deleted or refreshed within 30 days.
      • commentsThe first comments on a video, cheaply. No oldest-first order and no total; the pilot pages to the end to see how a thread began.

      04Reference

      The terms that bind the researcher

      Six passages from YouTube's Terms of Service, the API Developer Policies and the Data API documentation, verbatim with section numbers, as read on October 13, 2026, and the three texts that say who may ask.

      YouTube Terms of Service, Permissions and Restrictions, item 3
      "access the Service using any automated means (such as robots, botnets or scrapers) except (a) in the case of public search engines, in accordance with YouTube's robots.txt file; or (b) with YouTube's prior written permission"
      The clause that puts caption text, community posts and any collection outside the API off limits. The X decision found that a contractual ban of this kind cannot be applied to researchers covered by Art. 40(12). Exception (b) is the door Step 1 asks the Commission to open as a class.
      API Developer Policies III.E.4.b
      "To be clear, an API Client must not store statistics retrieved as Non-Authorized Data for more than 30 days."
      View, like and comment counts for videos the researcher does not own are non-authorized data. A series kept for six months exceeds this ceiling as written.
      API Developer Policies III.E.4.d
      "API Clients may temporarily store limited amounts of Non-Authorized Data for as long as is necessary for the purposes of the API Client but not longer than 30 calendar days."
      The same ceiling for every other field taken through the API, including titles and descriptions, unless the data is refreshed or deleted every 30 days.
      API Developer Policies III.E.2.b
      "Do not aggregate API Data or otherwise use API Data or YouTube API Services to gain insights into YouTube's usage, revenue, or any other aspects of YouTube's business."
      Cross-outlet research measures how the platform is used. Whether that falls under this clause is decided by the platform, which is the uncertainty Step 1 asks the Commission to remove.
      API Developer Policies III.E.6 and III.I.14
      "You and your API Clients must not, and must not encourage, enable, or require others to, directly or indirectly, scrape YouTube Applications or Google Applications, or obtain scraped YouTube data or content." / "use any technology other than YouTube API Services to access or retrieve API Data, including to access any portion of any YouTube audiovisual content"
      Together with item 3 of the Terms of Service, these close the only route to the text of a caption track for a video one does not own.
      Data API quota, Getting started
      "a default quota allocation of 100 search.list calls, 100 videos.insert calls, and 10,000 units per day combined for all other endpoints"; "All API requests, including invalid requests, incur at least a one-point quota cost." More is available through "the Quota extension request form for YouTube API Services."
      One night of the US monitoring used 3,366 units for metadata and the comments of six stories. Covering every comment on a day's 5,889 videos would not fit.

      Who may ask, in three texts

      • Art. 40(12) DSA. Access for "researchers, including those affiliated to not for profit bodies, organisations and associations, who comply with the conditions set out in paragraph 8, points (b), (c), (d) and (e)". Those points are independence from commercial interests, disclosure of funding, data security, and proportionality. No academic affiliation test, no residence test.
      • Google's DSA researcher programme page (transparency.google, read Oct 13, 2026). Lists the Art. 40(8) conditions and "affiliated with not for profit bodies, organizations and associations", then adds: "This programme is currently only available for researchers affiliated with an EU-based institution" and "Individual platform teams may have additional eligibility requirements beyond those listed here." Step 1 names the residence test as one the standard should remove; the X decision treated the bearing of the research on risks in the Union, not the researcher's seat, as the test.
      • YouTube Researcher Program (research.youtube, read Oct 13, 2026). Applicants must be "affiliated with an accredited, higher-education institution that can grant degrees"; the program is open in 60 countries, Ireland and 25 of the 27 EU member states among them (Croatia and Slovenia are not listed); "researchers will provide YouTube with free access to and use of all research based upon YouTube data".

      Limits

      • One platform, two days. YouTube only. X, Meta and TikTok are not measured here.
      • Chapter 01 is eight videos. The top video of each of the six October 12 stories and the two most-viewed Irish videos in the probe. The captions.download refusal was recorded on one track; the documentation states the same requirement for every video.
      • View growth is one interval. Readings 16.6 hours apart show that a series can only be built by polling; they say nothing about the shape between them.
      • Quota is counted by the client. The Google Cloud console was not consulted.
      • Terms are quoted, not interpreted. Whether cross-outlet research falls under III.E.2.b, and whether the X decision's reasoning transfers to YouTube, are questions for the Commission and the courts; the proposal asks for the first and assumes the second.
      • Estimates are marked. The planner's outputs are arithmetic on measured volumes and published costs; the measured US fetch cost 1,028 units where the same arithmetic gives 613, because high-volume channels need several playlist pages. Read planner outputs as a floor.

      Scripts and outputs

      • record_api_views.py produced api_record.json (chapter 01; comment text and author names removed).
      • probe_ie_channels.py and resolve_ie_search.py produced ie_probe.json and ie_search_resolved.json (chapter 03).
      • poll_views.py took the second reading of 586 videos; view_snapshots_aggregate.json holds the per-story totals and the growth distribution.
      • fetch_repeat.py re-read the card from the public API on October 26, 2026, matched the six videos of the repeated comment to their ids, and stored their thumbnails (videos.list, 1 unit) in repeat.json.
      • fetch_logos.py fetched the channel avatars shown in chapters 01 and 03 (videos.list and channels.list, 2 units, October 26, 2026) into channels.json; the avatars are stored with the page rather than loaded from the platform.
      • calc.py derives every figure on this page from those four files and writes facts.js; the counts that come from the pipeline's own run log (channels, videos fetched, comments counted and collected) are listed there with their source.
      • The nightly fetch and comment collection are pipeline code in the team's repository (07_phase2a_24h_fetch.py, collect_comments.py).
